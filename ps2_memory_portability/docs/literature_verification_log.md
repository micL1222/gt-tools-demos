## `jeon2023compatibility` — CORE

- **Authoritative records:** [American Economic Association article
  page](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/mic.20200309); [Toulouse
  School of Economics author-posted full-text
  version](https://www.tse-fr.eu/sites/default/files/TSE/documents/doc/by/jeon/compatibility_choices_01_2022.pdf).
- **Metadata checked:** Doh-Shin Jeon, Domenico Menicucci, **Nikrooz Nasr**;
  *American Economic Journal: Microeconomics* 15(1), February 2023, 30–73;
  DOI `10.1257/mic.20200309`.
- **Result/model checked:** two firms sell complementary products; dynamic
  setting; firms simultaneously choose compatibility/incompatibility at the
  beginning of period one; switching costs apply to later switching; analysis
  uses SPNE. High switching costs and patience can sustain incompatibility;
  data portability reduces switching costs and can induce compatibility more
  often. Consumer effects under a fixed regime depend on the nonnegative-price
  constraint.
- **Discrepancy:** the prompt listed “Nima Nasr.” The verified author is
  **Nikrooz Nasr**. The project comparison must also say dynamic SPNE, not
  static private-cost BNE.
- **Decision:** include as the closest strategic benchmark.

## `viard2007switching` — CORE

- **Authoritative record:** [Wiley article
  page](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1756-2171.2007.tb00049.x).
- **Metadata checked:** V. Brian Viard; *The RAND Journal of Economics* 38(1),
  March 2007, 146–163; DOI `10.1111/j.1756-2171.2007.tb00049.x`.
- **Result checked:** AT&T and MCI reduced toll-free-service prices in response
  to number portability; larger contracts had larger price declines; the paper
  interprets the evidence as lower switching costs increasing competition in
  that setting.
- **Discrepancy:** Wiley displays “First published: 16 September 2008” on the
  migrated page, but its issue and citation records place the article in March
  2007. The formal issue year, 2007, is used.
- **Decision:** include as an empirical analogy, with no transfer of magnitude
  to AI assistants.

## `kim2026portability` — CORE

- **Authoritative record:** [Wiley open-access article
  page](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jems.12643).
- **Metadata checked:** **Jeong-Yoo Kim**; *Journal of Economics & Management
  Strategy* 35(2), 219–232; DOI `10.1111/jems.12643`; first published online 21
  May 2025; formal issue year 2026.
- **Result/model checked:** two-period Hotelling duopoly; simultaneous price
  setting; switching costs, network benefits, rational expectations, breach
  risk, care, and liability. Without breaches, required portability and
  interoperability improve modeled welfare; with breaches, they raise accident
  probability, while the studied joint-and-several-liability/negligence regime
  can induce efficient care.
- **Discrepancy:** Crossref's early-publication metadata reports 2025, while the
  current Wiley version of record assigns volume 35, issue 2, pages 219–232 to
  2026. The bibliography uses **2026** and records 2025 in a note.
- **Decision:** include for digital-platform relevance and the security-risk
  boundary; never summarize it as unconditional welfare improvement.
